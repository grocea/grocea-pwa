# Grocea one-week trial kit

Version: 2026-10-16. This is a protocol and blank templates, not research results. Recruit 3–5 consenting adults only after an organizer has arranged the study directly. No invitations or messages are sent by this toolkit. Seven elapsed days and actual participation remain external requirements.

## Organizer preparation

Use participant codes (P01–P05) in study records; keep any scheduling/contact information separately. Explain the study purpose: learn whether setup, stock maintenance, shopping, cooking and trust are practical in an ordinary routine. Offer a demonstration or a disposable practice account. Each person uses their own account, browser and device; never share credentials. Do not ask for pantry screenshots, recipe contents, passwords, health information or household identities. Do not observe or record a screen without separate explicit consent. The trial journal is optional, off by default and separate from permission to take part in an interview.

Before recruiting, fill in organizer/contact, study dates, interview method, where manually shared exports will be stored, who can access them, retention/deletion date, and how to withdraw shared data before de-identification. Do not promise withdrawal of already aggregated anonymous observations. Agree whether any compensation applies and that withdrawal does not remove it. Do not collect exports until this information has been explained and accepted.

## Session and diary plan

Day 1 (15–25 minutes): Sign in online and finish first sync. Review privacy/consent, optionally start the journal. Add a small amount of real or practice pantry stock with metric quantities. Check the resulting balance; try bulk setup if useful. Ask how they know it saved and whether the quantity/unit feels clear. Do not require an exact pantry inventory.

Days 2–3: When convenient, start a standalone shopping list or add a restock item; check items off and finish shopping. Ask what “complete” means and whether the pantry change matches expectations. If offline, explain that local success and server acceptance are different. Do not encourage buying anything solely for the study.

Days 4–5: If a recipe fits their routine, use cook preview and confirm cooking; otherwise skip and record why. Ask whether stock deductions and immutable history are understandable. Do not require cooking or consuming anything solely for the study.

Days 6–7: Return on a later day, inspect stock/restock preferences and update a balance if needed. Try an offline edit only if comfortable after the first online sync. Review pending/sync issue status on reconnect. End after seven actual elapsed days, review notes/export, and hold a 15-minute interview. Stop recording when participation ends. Exporting is optional; clear the journal whenever requested.

## Outcome definitions and interpretation

Automatic local journal fields: consent version, start/stop times and start local date, coarse accepted milestone names with action/acknowledgement times, distinct later local dates, and participant-entered notes. Internal consent-period and opaque mutation IDs support pending/duplicate handling but are excluded from export. No automatic account identity, pantry/recipe payloads, amounts or labels are exported. The export does contain arbitrary note text; review before sharing.

| Question | Evidence and definition | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can people set up a pantry? | First server-acknowledged stock operation/bulk save during the consent period; ask whether setup felt complete. | A single stock save is a setup proxy, not a complete inventory or observed usability success. |
| Can they maintain quantities efficiently? | Diary effort, confusion, assistance and workarounds around bulk/pack entry. | No automatic duration or pack-use tracking; self-report only. |
| Can shopping stand on its own? | Server-acknowledged shopping completion plus interview on standalone/restock route. | Completion does not identify which route was used or prove a purchase. |
| Does cooking connect to stock as expected? | Server-acknowledged cooking completion plus confidence/preview discussion. | It does not prove a meal was made or eaten. |
| Do restock preferences help? | Diary/interview about thresholds and preferred refill amounts. | No automatic preference payloads or reminders collected. |
| Do offline/recovery/data controls feel trustworthy? | Ask how pending/accepted states, recovery, export and deletion were understood; optional organizer-observed practice tasks. | No automatic recovery/deletion telemetry; do not delete a participant’s account for evaluation. |
| Do participants return? | One record per distinct local date after the start date on a foreground visit or interaction. | Same-day reloads do not count; device clock changes, multiple devices and local data loss limit this proxy. |

Offline candidates are created only under the consent period active when an action is initiated, and only after durable local save. They are not accepted outcomes. A server acknowledgement promotes the candidate; stopping, clearing, rejection, conflict or discard invalidates it. Pre-consent actions never count when their queue later drains. Journal storage failures pause collection without failing kitchen work; report missing data rather than interpreting absence as non-use. The journal can run longer than seven days; the organizer sets the actual observation window and stops it. It is not an automatic study timer.

## Exit interview template

1. What did you use Grocea for, and what did you skip? Why?
2. Where did setup or keeping stock current take too much effort?
3. How did standalone shopping and restock-to-list fit your routine?
4. Did cook preview and resulting stock changes match expectations?
5. Were metric amounts, pack arithmetic and low-stock settings understandable?
6. How did you tell the difference between saved locally, pending and accepted by the server?
7. What would you expect from recovery, data export, deletion and journal controls?
8. What would make you choose to return next week? What would make you stop?
9. What is the one change you would prioritize? Any privacy concerns?

Record observed behavior, participant quotations (with permission), self-report and organizer inference separately. Do not infer a cause from a milestone alone.

## Review rubric and blank synthesis

For each broad task, record independently completed / assisted / workaround / skipped / failed; severity: blocking / major friction / minor friction. Tie each finding to a participant code, date and concrete evidence; distinguish a broken workflow from preference. Count people, not repeated clicks. State denominators, consent window, missing exports, storage failures and skipped tasks. With 3–5 people, report qualitative patterns and exceptions; do not claim statistical proof, savings or waste reduction. Prioritize reproducible blockers, then repeated effort/trust problems; propose a small fix and a follow-up check.
